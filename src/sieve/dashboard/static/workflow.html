How SIEVE works

A team of AI agents decides which of 8,803 MOFs to simulate, and how carefully, on a fixed 4 CPU-hour budget. Independent checks then decide whether to believe the answer.

      What stays with the scientist

      • The model. Force field, charges or none, rigid framework, temperature, pressure: the agents optimise within it, never question it.
      • The question. Uptake at 1 bar or a flue-gas capture score; the budget; which pool.
      • Reading the caveats. No charges, no water, disorder, pore blocking: the tool flags them, a person decides what they mean.
      • The literature and the lab. Looking up a lead in the CSD and its paper, then making and measuring it.

      4 · Agents that spawn agents three places where an agent splits its job and hands the parts to children

      Scout

      • Scout picks families to study
        • sub-scout · family f1 search rule + promise
          • sub-sub-scout · f1a
          • sub-sub-scout · f1b
        • sub-scout · family f2
        • sub-scout · family f3 …

      If a sub-scout finds its family is really two kinds of structure, it splits the family and hands each half to a child.

      Critic

      • Critic audits the ranking
        • sub-critic · leader 1 credible? confirm?
        • sub-critic · leader 2
        • sub-critic · leader 3

      Each contested leader gets its own examiner, which compares it with what similar structures measured. The critic rules with their verdicts in hand.

      Peer reviewer

      • Lead reviewer collects every claim
        • sub-reviewer · claim 1 supported?
        • sub-reviewer · claim 2 compound: split
          • part a
          • part b
        • sub-reviewer · claim 3 …

      A claim joining two assertions ("X holds a lot because of Y") is split, and each part is judged on its own. Verdicts roll up in code, not by vote.

      • Bounded. At most two levels below any agent (depth ≤ 2), so a run can never spawn without limit.
      • Parallel. Siblings run at the same time; the parent waits for all of them, then decides.
      • Cheap children. Children use a smaller model where that is enough; the parent keeps the big one.
      • Never stuck. If a child's model call fails, a rule-based fallback answers, and the event log says so.

      The rules that keep it honest

      • Cheap runs only rank. Tier-1 simulations are short and unconverged (±18%). They decide what to look at, never what wins.
      • A winner needs a confirmed run. The critic vetoes any structure without a tier-2 simulation (±8.6%).
      • No AI sees the answers. Agents see only descriptors and their own measurements, never ground truth.
      • Every claim is checked against data. The peer reviewer splits compound claims and judges each part. Claims the model cannot test (charges, open metal sites) are never marked supported.
      • Losses are reported. When a baseline beats SIEVE, the Compare page says so first.
      • Crashes are normal. Every job and decision is in a database, so python -m sieve resume continues after a kill -9.
