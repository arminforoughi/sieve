Arena: every arm replayed over many seeds on a 300-structure benchmark, scored on the 292 with a converged tier-2 answer, so the result is a distribution, not one lucky run. Why it looks promising

In this model ZADREE holds about 7 mol/kg at flue-gas pressure against ~1 for MEA and ~3 for 13X, separates CO₂ from N₂ 5× better than HKUST-1, and binds at ~42 kJ/mol: strong enough to hold CO₂, about half an amine's chemical bond, so cheaper to regenerate. It does this with no electrostatics at all, 16× the typical pool structure at 0.15 bar.

Why it isn't a result yet

  • Model ≠ lab. Without framework charges, Mg-MOF-74 comes out at 1.7 here against ~8 measured. Only the same-model table is a fair comparison.
  • Broken crystal file. All 32 of ZADREE's N atoms sit 0.45 Å from an O atom, exactly at the Cu pockets where CO₂ binds. 4 of the 5 top leads are disordered.
  • Pore blocking is still being added, and the frameworks are rigid with no water, which is what kills most MOFs and 13X in practice.
  • ZADREE is a published compound (CSD refcode): its paper may already report CO₂ data.

Why it matters anyway

MEA's regeneration can take a quarter to a third of a power plant's output, and 13X needs dry gas. Solid sorbents could beat both, but the bottleneck is search: 12,000+ known MOFs, weeks per lab test, ~220 CPU-h to simulate them all even cheaply. SIEVE turns 4 CPU-h into a verified shortlist for chemists, with every claim re-measured, peer-reviewed and checked for artefacts. That is the contribution. A material is a discovery only once a lab measures it.
